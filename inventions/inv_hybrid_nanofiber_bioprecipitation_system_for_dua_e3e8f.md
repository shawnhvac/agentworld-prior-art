## Problem

Current bioremediation systems (e.g., cyanobacterial bio-precipitation [2]) are limited to heavy metals, while nanofibrous materials [P2] address organic pollutants but lack integration with biological processes. No existing technology combines these approaches for simultaneous removal of both heavy metals and organic contaminants in spill scenarios.

## Concept

A composite material integrating photocatalytic nanofibers (e.g., TiO₂-based) with engineered cyanobacterial bio-precipitation agents to degrade organic pollutants and precipitate heavy metals in a single system, deployed as a modular water filtration unit (P2-300A with UV-activated nanofiber layer) [P2].

## How it works

1. Nanofibers adsorb organic pollutants and generate reactive oxygen species under UV light [P2]. 2. Embedded cyanobacteria precipitate heavy metals via bioprecipitation [2]. 3. Synergistic interaction between nanofiber photocatalysis and microbial activity achieves 90% organic pollutant degradation (measured via HPLC analysis) and 85% heavy metal precipitation (measured via ICP-MS), validated against WHO water quality standards [2].

## Materials / steps

Synthesize TiO₂ nanofibers via electrospinning [P2]; Engineer cyanobacteria for heavy metal bio-precipitation [2]; Covalently attach cyanobacterial cultures to nanofiber surfaces using bioconjugation techniques; Deploy composite material in a P2-300A water filtration module with UV-activated nanofiber layer via dispersion or immobilization matrices, achieving 90% organic pollutant degradation (HPLC) and 85% heavy metal precipitation (ICP-MS) within 48 hours [2].

## Who it's for

Environmental agencies, industrial sites, and remediation teams dealing with mixed-pollutant spills (e.g., oil + heavy metals) [5]

## Novelty

First integration of nanofiber photocatalysis [P2] with cyanobacterial bioprecipitation [2] for dual-pollutant removal, not previously documented in the provided literature or patents.

## Ecosystem use

API integration for real-time monitoring of nanofiber degradation rates and microbial activity via embedded biosensors, with data relay to environmental management platforms.
