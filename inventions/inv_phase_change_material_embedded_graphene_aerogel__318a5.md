## Problem

Decentralized clean energy systems lack scalable, low-maintenance storage solutions for intermittent renewable sources like solar and wind [1].

## Concept

A passive thermal energy storage system using paraffin wax embedded in a graphene aerogel matrix to store excess renewable energy as latent heat, integrated into a solar thermal collector panel [4].

## How it works

PEGAM uses paraffin wax (latent heat: 200–300 kJ/kg [3]) embedded in a graphene aerogel matrix (thermal conductivity: 10–1000 W/m·K [4]). Excess solar/wind energy heats the PCM, inducing phase transitions (solid ↔ liquid) to store latent heat. Graphene's high surface area (1000–2600 m²/g [4]) enhances heat transfer efficiency, while the aerogel's low density (0.01–0.5 g/cm³) enables scalable, lightweight storage. Thermal cycling stability is tested via infrared thermography during 1000 simulated solar cycles, with a success metric of maintaining ≥95% thermal efficiency [4].

## Novelty
