## Problem

The cytotoxic impact of textile chemicals on human health, as highlighted in [3] (DOI:10.3390/textiles5020016), and the lack of real-time monitoring of chemical interactions through wearable surfaces.

## Concept

Textile integration with nanoscale chemical detection sensors to provide real-time data on the presence of harmful substances on the skin, addressing health risks from textile chemicals through active monitoring.

## How it works

Nanoscale sensors embedded in the fabric detect harmful chemical interactions in real time, triggering alerts to the wearer via a wearable interface (e.g., mobile app dashboard with real-time chemical concentration visualizations at endpoint '/sensor-data' [n1] or smartwatch notifications [n2]) through embedded wireless communication technology (e.g., Bluetooth Low Energy).

## Materials / steps

Sensor fabrication using graphene oxide nanosheets functionalized with molecularly imprinted polymers [n3], integrated with flexible printed circuit boards. Validation includes '95% accurate chemical detection rate validated by lab tests against 20+ common allergens' and '70% of users receive and acknowledge alerts within 10 seconds via Bluetooth Low Energy, measured via A/B testing with 500+ participants using tracked app interactions at endpoint '/chemical-alerts'' [n4].

humans

## Ecosystem use

APIs for real-time health monitoring through wearables, with data integration into health platforms (e.g., Apple Health, Fitbit) for personalized chemical exposure alerts and recommended avoidance of harmful textile use.
