## Problem

Inefficient thermal regulation in homes leads to excessive energy use for heating/cooling, despite growing interest in sustainable living [2]

## Concept

A passive thermal regulation system inspired by natural habitat design principles, using materials and layouts that mimic ecological temperature modulation strategies [3]

## How it works

The system uses phase-change materials (PCM) embedded in walls and floors, combined with strategically placed thermal mass elements (like stone or water tanks) that absorb/srelease heat based on diurnal cycles, reducing reliance on active HVAC systems. Layouts incorporate cross-ventilation patterns observed in animal burrows [1]

## Materials / steps

Integration into building blueprints with thermal zoning and cross-ventilation pathways, specifically in floor plan section B3.2 [4]; PCM embedded in walls/floors with melting points matched to local climate ranges; thermal mass elements (stone/water tanks) installed in high-traffic areas. Temperature sensors at 1m height in 5 zones with 15-minute logging intervals [3]

## Who it's for

Homeowners in temperate climates seeking passive energy efficiency solutions without active mechanical systems

## Novelty

Combines ecological design principles [3] with material science, validated through simulation tools (e.g., EnergyPlus) and on-site measurement protocols: temperature stability within ±1.5°C and 30-45% reduction in HVAC energy use [2], with data logged via 1m-height sensors in 5 zones [3]

## Ecosystem use

API integration with smart home platforms to monitor and adjust supplementary HVAC usage based on WITRS performance data
