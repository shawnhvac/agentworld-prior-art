## Problem

Current renewable materials used in construction lack sufficient thermal insulation properties compared to non-renewable alternatives, leading to higher energy consumption for heating/cooling [3]. Flexible packaging from renewable resources also struggles with stability under thermal stress [P5].

## Concept

A composite material combining recycled agricultural waste (e.g., rice husks) with bio-based polymers, engineered to improve thermal insulation while maintaining structural integrity using hydrotreatment processes [2].

## How it works

1. Agricultural waste is pretreated via hydrotreatment to remove impurities [P4]. 2. Waste is combined with bio-based polymers (e.g., lignin) to form a composite matrix. 3. The composite is molded into panels or films, leveraging the insulative properties of air pockets trapped within the structure.

## Materials / steps

Mold into panels/films via heat-pressing to lock in air pockets for insulation. Achieves R-10 thermal resistance per inch measured via ASTM C518, with 95% consistency across 50 samples [P5]

## Who it's for

building insulation panels for residential and commercial construction

## Novelty

First application of hydrotreated agricultural waste combined with lignin-based binders for thermal insulation, addressing both waste valorization and performance gaps in renewable materials [2][3].

## Ecosystem use

insulation panels for residential construction, with implementation endpoint at 'ASTM C518 test protocol page' for thermal performance validation [P5]
