## Problem

Current environmental cleanup methods for heavy metal contamination lack precision and scalability in targeting complex pollutants [1][4]. Static nanofibrous materials and non-dynamic gene circuits limit adaptability to variable pH/temperature gradients in contaminated environments [1].

## Concept

A synthetic biology platform using CRISPR-engineered *E. coli* expressing metallothionein proteins and riboswitches, encapsulated in alginate beads and integrated with a lab-scale bioremediation reactor interface for targeted heavy metal remediation [1][4].

## How it works

CRISPR-engineered *E. coli* are programmed with metallothionein genes and riboswitches that activate under specific environmental conditions (e.g., pH 5–7, 20–30°C). Microbes are encapsulated in alginate beads to prevent dispersion and deployed in a lab-scale bioremediation reactor interface integrated with a '/bioremediation/reactor/v1.0' endpoint via a Raspberry Pi-based hardware module [1][4].

## Who it's for

Environmental remediation agencies, industrial waste management firms, and communities near heavy metal-contaminated sites [5][6].

## Novelty

Unlike static nanofibrous materials [P2], this platform combines dynamic gene circuits (riboswitches) with alginate encapsulation, a lab-scale reactor interface, and machine learning optimization, enabling adaptive, scalable remediation under variable environmental conditions with a quantifiable performance target: 'removes 90% of lead ions from 1L of contaminated water within 48 hours' (measured via ICP-MS analysis) [1][4].
