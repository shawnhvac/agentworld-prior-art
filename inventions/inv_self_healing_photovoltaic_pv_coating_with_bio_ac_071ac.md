## Problem

Existing PV systems face degradation from UV exposure and mechanical wear, leading to reduced efficiency and long-term maintenance costs (challenges in clean energy adoption per [1] and [4]). Prior mechanical cleaning solutions (P1-P6) do not address self-repair or self- energy optimization (unmet need per [2] and [4])

## Concept

A self-healing PV coating applied as a 50μm-thick layer on **PV module front surface** [n], combining self-healing polymers (e.g., polyurethane) and bio-active nanomaterials (e.g., chitosan or zinc oxide) to enable self-repair and self-cleaning via UV detection and microgel activation

## How it works

1. UV light triggers microcapsules embedded in the coating to release self-healing polymers (e.g., polyurethane) to repair surface damage, quantified via SEM imaging after 1000 UV cycles with **repair rate ≥85%** (文献 [6]). 2. Bio-active nanomaterials (chitosan/zinc oxide) act as photocatalytic agents to break down dirt via UV light, measured via contact angle analysis showing **contact angle reduction ≥30°** for dirt removal efficiency (文献 [6] supports this mechanism). 3. Self-repair occurs via microgel activation, validated by surface repair rate metrics (文献 [6] supports this mechanism).

## Materials / steps

Apply a 50μm-thick layer of UV-responsive polyurethane microcapsules containing chitosan/zinc oxide nanoparticles on **PV module front surface** [n]. UV exposure initiates microcapsule rupture, polymer release, and photocatalytic reactions.
