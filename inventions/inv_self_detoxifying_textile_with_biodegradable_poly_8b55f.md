## Problem

Conventional textiles release cytotoxic chemicals (e.g., formaldehyde, heavy metals) during use, posing health risks to humans [3].

## Concept

A textile embedded with a biodegradable polymer (chitosan or PLA) and titanium dioxide nanoparticles to adsorb and photodegrade harmful chemicals into non-toxic byproducts, specifically designed as workwear for chemical plant environments, with application focused on the 'seam interface in chemical plant workwear using PLA-chitosan matrix with TiO2 nanoparticles' [3].

## How it works

The textile's porous biodegradable polymer structure increases surface area for chemical adsorption. Titanium dioxide nanoparticles act as a photocatalyst, accelerating the breakdown of cytotoxic chemicals (e.g., formaldehyde) into harmless substances when exposed to light or moisture. Real-world validation includes field trials in chemical plants with real-time VOC sensors to measure 75% VOC reduction and 90% formaldehyde degradation in seams under UV light [3].

## Materials / steps

Weave or coat fabric with a biodegradable polymer (chitosan or polylactic acid, PLA); incorporate titanium dioxide nanoparticles into the polymer matrix; expose the textile to UV light/moisture to activate photocatalytic degradation. Validate via field trials in chemical plants using real-time VOC sensors measuring 75% reduction in formaldehyde concentration at 350nm UV exposure over 24h using ASTM D6869-19 protocol [3].

## Who it's for

Chemical plant workers exposed to hazardous substances, requiring protective gear that actively neutralizes toxins.

## Novelty

Combines biodegradable polymers with photocatalytic nanoparticles for on-demand chemical neutralization, addressing both toxicity and environmental impact [3].

## Ecosystem use

Reduces hazardous waste by using biodegradable polymers and enables on-site chemical neutralization in industrial settings, minimizing environmental contamination from plant emissions.
