## Problem

Microbial biofilm growth in HVAC ducts causes persistent health risks (allergies, infections) and system inefficiency (reduced airflow, mold damage), yet existing systems lack targeted solutions for this biological contamination [1][6].

## Concept

A **Photocatalytic UV-C Duct Coating System** that uses TiO₂ nanocoatings on **air handler duct interior walls** (e.g., air handler ducts), activated by embedded UV-C LEDs, to continuously oxidize organic matter and disrupt microbial biofilms.

## How it works

TiO₂ nanoparticles (anatase, 21 nm) are applied via sol-gel coating to **air handler duct interior walls**. Embedded UV-C LEDs (280 nm wavelength) emit light that activates TiO₂, generating electron-hole pairs. These produce reactive oxygen species (superoxide radicals and hydroxyl radicals) that oxidize biofilm components (proteins, polysaccharides) and disrupt microbial cell membranes [1].

## Materials / steps

TiO₂ nanoparticles (anatase, 21 nm particle size) from Sigma-Aldrich; Sol-gel coating process for TiO₂ application to duct surfaces; UV-C LEDs (280 nm wavelength) from OSRAM, mounted on flexible LED strips; Duct cleaning with isopropyl alcohol prior to coating; Installation of LED strips along **air handler duct interior walls**, spaced 15 cm apart for uniform UV-C exposure; Post-implementation verification via **weekly swab tests using ISO 16231-1:2016 protocol**, showing 50% reduction in CFU/m² compared to pre-implementation baselines [2].

## Who it's for

HVAC maintenance professionals, building managers, and occupants of commercial/industrial facilities with duct systems prone to microbial contamination (e.g., hospitals, schools, food processing plants).

## Novelty

First-of-its-kind integration of photocatalytic materials (TiO₂) with UV-C LEDs in HVAC ducts to target microbial biofilms—a problem unaddressed in prior art [P1–P6]. Unlike refrigerant-focused or control-logic systems, this addresses biological contamination directly.
