## Problem

Dusty environments reduce photovoltaic (PV) panel efficiency, but current solutions rely on water-intensive processes [4] or mechanical scrubbing that risks panel damage [P4], hindering clean energy adoption in arid regions [1]

## Concept

A passive photocatalytic self-cleaning film applied to the 'panel/glass/cleaning-protocol/v2.1/photocatalytic-layer' that decomposes organic soot and dirt via UV-activated TiO₂ nanocapillaries [2], enabling real-time efficiency tracking through API v2.1

## How it works

TiO₂ nanocapillary layer (ALD-deposited on amorphous silicate polymer) generates ·OH radicals under UV light to break down organic dirt [2]. Sputtered ZnO nanowires (upper surface) create hydrophilic gradient; perfluoroalkyl groups (lower surface) enable water droplet pinning and passive runoff [5]. Success is quantified by 30% reduction in organic residue weight after 72h UV exposure, logged via API v2.1/sensors/residue-logs

## Materials / steps

Integration of ALD-TiO₂ nanocapillaries with ZnO nanowires and perfluoroalkyl surface patterning for synergistic photocatalytic cleaning and gradient-driven runoff on photovoltaic panel glass layer API v2.1

## Who it's for

PV panel manufacturers and operators in arid regions (e.g., Middle East, Sub-Saharan Africa), where dust accumulation is common and water access is limited [4]
