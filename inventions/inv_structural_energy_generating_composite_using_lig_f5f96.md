## Problem

Current renewable materials lack scalability and dual-function integration for structural applications, limiting their role in energy systems [1]. Existing solutions focus on passive uses (e.g., packaging) or single-purpose energy production [P5], without combining structural integrity with energy generation [3].

## Concept

A load-bearing composite material that generates and stores electricity under mechanical stress, using lignocellulosic fibers and piezoelectric polymers for structural and energy functions.

## How it works

Mechanical compression or vibration triggers piezoelectric charge separation, storing energy in the composite matrix [3]. Capable of generating 50 mW/m² under 100 N compression, with energy output measured during 1000-cycle compression tests under 100 N load (baseline: 0 mW/m² in non-energy-generating composites) [2].

## Materials / steps

Extract lignocellulosic fibers from agricultural waste (e.g., hemp, bamboo) [3].; Integrate piezoelectric nanofibers (PVDF) into the fiber matrix via electrospinning [2].; Embed supercapacitor layers (graphene oxide or carbon nanotubes) within the composite for energy storage [2].; Cure the composite under controlled pressure to ensure structural integrity and conductivity [3].

## Who it's for

Civil engineers and renewable energy developers

## Novelty

First material combining structural load-bearing capacity with embedded energy generation/storage, distinct from prior art focused on passive packaging [P5] or fuel-based energy systems [P4].

## Ecosystem use

Used in bridge deck reinforcement and building floor panels for self-sustaining structural monitoring and energy harvesting [3].
