## Problem

Inefficient recovery of trace elements (e.g., Fe, Zn) from mixed waste streams in closed-loop systems (e.g., space habitats, resource-scarce regions) compromises long-term sustainability, as highlighted by the necessity of closed ecological life support systems (CELSS) for trace element recycling [2].

## Concept

A biohybrid system combining AI-driven sorting with engineered microbial consortia to solubilize and recover trace elements from heterogeneous waste, leveraging bioavailability principles [2] and AI optimization [3].

## How it works

1. Microbial consortia solubilize trace metals from waste via biofilm-mediated reactions. 2. AI sensors analyze waste composition and optimize pH/oxidation conditions through the 'AI waste analysis dashboard' endpoint [3]. 3. Electrochemical modules recover solubilized metals using the 'electrochemical recovery module API' [4]. 4. Recovered elements are purified and reintegrated into material/nutrient cycles.

## Materials / steps

Engineered microbial consortia (selected for metal solubilization, per [2]); pH/oxidation sensors and AI-driven control algorithms (based on [3]); Electrochemical recovery cells with selective membranes; Synthetic mixed-waste matrices (Fe/Zn-containing simulants from [2]); Measurable checks: 'metal recovery rate >85% by mass spectrometry' and 'pH stabilization within ±0.2 units' [2].

## Who it's for

Space habitats requiring closed-loop resource management [2], and terrestrial regions with limited access to trace elements (e.g., arid areas, conflict zones) [1].

## Novelty

Unlike prior art focused on polymer recycling [P1–P2] or QR code-based sorting [P3], this system integrates microbial solubilization with AI-optimized electrochemical recovery for trace elements, addressing a gap in CELSS and resource-scarce contexts [2].
