## Problem

Contamination by waterborne parasites (e.g., trematodiases [1]) and fungal pathogens (e.g., Phoma spp. [4]) in food and water sources, which pose health risks due to their interdependency in human intake [3].

## Concept

A thin, edible antimicrobial film applied to specific surfaces such as 'plastic water bottle surface' or 'fresh produce surface', using plant-derived polyphenols to inhibit microbial growth without affecting human consumption.

## How it works

The film is coated with polyphenols (e.g., from green tea or grape seeds) that disrupt microbial cell membranes through oxidative stress and metal ion chelation [4]. It releases 5–10 μg/cm² of active compounds over 24 hours, reducing bacterial load by 90% in 24h and extending shelf life by 50% [5]. Bacterial load reduction is measured using ASTM E2182-18 protocol [6].

## Materials / steps

Source plant-based polyphenols (e.g., green tea extract) from sustainable sources; formulate a biodegradable polymer matrix (e.g., alginate or chitosan) to encapsulate polyphenols; apply the mixture as a thin, edible film (50–100 μm thickness) using spin-coating or dip-coating techniques onto 'plastic water bottle surface' or 'fresh produce surface'; test film stability under food/water storage conditions (pH 3–8, 4°C–30°C) and measure microbial load reduction using ASTM E2182-18 protocol [5,6].

## Who it's for

Individuals and communities in regions with limited access to clean water and safe food preservation methods, particularly in low-resource settings.

## Novelty

Unlike prior art focused on detection [P4], this invention prevents contamination at the source by leveraging the interdependency of food and water [3] with a dual-purpose material.
