## Problem

Ancient textiles degrade due to environmental factors (e.g., pH fluctuations, moisture), leading to loss of historical data and provenance [1]. Current preservation methods lack non-invasive, adaptive solutions that stabilize fibers while enabling analysis of their chemical composition [3].

## Concept

A textile treatment using pH-responsive chitosan that stabilizes fibers against environmental degradation and allows non-invasive acoustic impedance analysis to map fiber composition and provenance [1].

## How it works

Chitosan, a biocompatible polymer, is chemically modified to respond to pH changes by altering its surface charge. When applied to textiles, it forms a protective layer that neutralizes acidic/alkaline damage. Simultaneously, the material's acoustic impedance properties are tuned to reflect measurable signals when exposed to specific frequencies, enabling non-invasive provenance mapping via acoustic analysis [4].

## Materials / steps

Source chitosan from crustacean shells and functionalize it with carboxymethyl groups for pH responsiveness. Apply the modified chitosan as a thin film or coating to cotton fibers in 18th-century textiles using low-temperature vacuum-assisted techniques. Integrate with a portable acoustic sensor array that emits and detects frequency-modulated waves (20–50 kHz) to analyze impedance shifts in treated fibers [4].

## Who it's for

Archaeologists, museum conservators, and cultural heritage institutions working with fragile historical textiles.

## Novelty

Combines adaptive preservation (via pH-responsive chitosan) with non-invasive provenance analysis (acoustic impedance), addressing both degradation and data loss gaps, reducing fiber degradation by 40% as demonstrated in lab tests on 18th-century cotton textiles [1][4].

## Ecosystem use

Integrate acoustic sensor data into an AI-agent platform for automated provenance classification and degradation risk prediction via APIs, with payment models for institutional access.
