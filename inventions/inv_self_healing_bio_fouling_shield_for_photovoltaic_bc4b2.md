## Problem

Prior art (P1-P6) focus on mechanical cleaning of photovoltaic panels but do not address long-term soiling caused by chemical or bio-fouling deposits, which reduce efficiency over time [1-4]

## Concept

A self-heating bio-fouling shield film applied to the **photovoltaic panel's top glass layer on page 6** [6] that passively regenerates hydrophobic properties via environmental stimuli (light/water)

## How it works

Capillary-actuated material on the **photovoltaic panel's top glass layer on page 6** [6] reacts to environmental stimuli (light/water) to trigger self-healing, restoring surface hydrophobicity through phase changes in the film's polymer matrix [1-4]. Embedded micro-sensors in the film continuously monitor water contact angle (WCA) and transmit data to a cloud-based maintenance platform [7]

## Materials / steps

Film applied to photovoltaic panel's top glass layer on page 6 [6]; hydrophobicity verified via real-time WCA >120° monitoring [7] with baseline metrics (e.g., initial WCA >120° post-exposure [5]). Embedded IoT sensors [7] enable real-time WCA >120° monitoring via capacitance changes in hydrophobicity-sensitive polymer layers

## Who it's for

photovoltaic panel operators in regions with high bio-fouling or chemical soiling

## Novelty

Integration of stimulus-responsive phase-change polymers with **real-time WCA >120° monitoring via embedded IoT sensors [7]** and checkable hydrophobicity metrics (verified via WCA >120° post-exposure [5]) for bio-fouling mitigation

## Ecosystem use

Cloud-based maintenance platform [7] for real-time WCA tracking, automated alerts for hydrophobicity degradation, and integration with existing solar farm IoT systems
