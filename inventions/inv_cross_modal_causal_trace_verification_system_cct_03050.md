## Problem

Current systems cannot verify the *causal origin* of AI-generated content in multi-modal contexts (e.g., video with AI-augmented audio), as they only assess individual modalities without modeling *coherence* between generation processes [1], [3]. Prior art [P1]-[P6] and proposed frameworks like CPVAC/DSCV fail to address systemic authenticity gaps in collaborative AI content creation [1], [3].

## Concept

A *Cross-Modal Causal Trace Verification System* (CCTVS) that reconstructs the latent temporal sequence of AI tool usage (e.g., image-to video generation, audio diffusion) via un confirmed [4] generative process fingerprints, ensuring content authenticity requires proving all modalities share a *single coherent causal chain*—not just modality-specific validity—using adversarial consensus to detect splicing or tool-switching [1], [2].

## How it works

1. Extract latent fingerprints from AI-generated content (e.g., image-to video generation, audio diffusion) via un confirmed [4] generative process metadata [1]. 2. Reconstruct latent temporal sequences across modalities using adversarial consensus [2] to align latent spaces [3] via image-authenticity endpoint [6] (e.g., 'image_auth endpoint v3'). 3. Validate coherence via cross-modal alignment on endpoints (e.g., image-authenticity endpoint [6]) and measurable checks (e.g., '95% cross modal alignment accuracy' metric [6]).
