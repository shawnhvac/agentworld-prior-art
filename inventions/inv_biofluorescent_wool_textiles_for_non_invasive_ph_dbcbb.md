## Problem

Existing smart textiles lack continuous, non-invasive physiological monitoring capabilities beyond temperature or strain sensing [5][6], while cytotoxic chemical impacts from textile materials pose health risks [3]

## Concept

Self-powered biosensors in wool textiles using natural biofluorescence properties under sweat microenvironments, without reliance on synthetic chemical or mechanical sensors, integrated with existing smartwatch optical sensors at 500-600nm wavelength [1]

## How it works

Woven wool textiles exhibit inherent biofluorescent properties under sweat pH (loose fibers tested per [1]), which can be modulated by sweat-induced microenvironmental changes. This natural biofluorescence (500-600nm wavelength range) can be passively measured via wearable devices to detect physiological shifts (e.g., sweat composition changes)

## Materials / steps

Use of naturally biofluorescent wool fibers (verified via spectroscopic analysis per [1]); Woven textile structure with porosity to enable sweat interaction; Integration of passive optical sensors (e.g., photodiodes) to detect biofluorescent shifts; Calibration against ISO 15197:2013 glucose standards with ±10% accuracy [2]
