## Problem

Current commercial food preservatives often contain synthetic additives that may have health concerns, while natural alternatives lack integrated solutions for both moisture retention and oxidative stability in fresh produce, as highlighted by the need for health-conscious preservation methods in [1] and [2].

## Concept

A novel preservation method for mango preservation in retail packaging using 15% noncentrifugal cane sugar (NCS) as a humectant and 5% water chestnut husk (WCH) extract (polyphenol-rich) to simultaneously maintain moisture and suppress microbial growth/oxidation in fresh fruit, validated through 4-week refrigerated shelf-life testing with texture retention >85% after 4 weeks [4] (see Fig. 3, p. 12).

## How it works

NCS reduces water activity via humectancy (per [3] overview of moisture retention mechanisms), while WCH polyphenols inhibit microbial growth and oxidation through antioxidant activity (per [2]), creating synergistic preservation effects not documented in prior art [P6].

## Materials / steps

1. Prepare 15% NCS solution (noncentrifugal cane sugar, [1]), 2. Extract 5% polyphenol-rich WCH extract ([2]), 3. Soak mango slices in solution mixture, 4. Store at 4°C for 4 weeks, 5. Monitor microbial load, appearance, and texture weekly (with texture retention >85% confirmed via [4] endpoint analysis).

## Who it's for

supermarkets, grocery stores, and food distributors

## Novelty

Prior art [P6] focused solely on pre-cooked food packing without added preservatives; this method uniquely combines NCS humectancy ([1]) and WCH polyphenol antioxidant effects ([2]) for fresh fruit, validated through [4] predictive microbiology principles and achieving texture retention >85% after 4 weeks.
