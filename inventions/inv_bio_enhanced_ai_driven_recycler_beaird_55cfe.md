## Problem

Inefficient recovery of trace elements and persistent polymer waste in recycling systems undermines sustainability goals, particularly for materials like polystyrene and mixed polymer composites [1][2].

## Concept

A hybrid system combining AI optimization with engineered microbial consortia (e.g., *Pseudomonas putida* and *Bacillus subtilis*) to improve trace element recovery and polymer breakdown in recycling processes, with real-time monitoring at bioreactor endpoint /bioreactor/monitoring [4].

## How it works

BEAIRD uses AI (e.g., LSTM networks) to analyze real-time sensor data (pH, temperature, polymer composition) from bioreactors and adjust conditions for microbial consortia. AI optimizes nutrient fluxes and metabolic pathways, while mechanical preprocessing (e.g., shredding at /preprocessing/shredding) and chemical treatments (e.g., solvents at /solvent/treatment) handle non-biodegradable fractions. Polymer degradation rates are tracked via FTIR scans at 10-minute intervals, with a 25% increase in degradation rate compared to baseline mechanical recycling [4].

## Materials / steps

Collect mixed polymer waste and separate non-biodegradable fractions via mechanical shredding at /preprocessing/shredding and chemical solvents at /solvent/treatment [4]; feed biodegradable fractions into bioreactors with engineered microbial consortia; use AI to monitor and adjust bioreactor conditions (pH, temperature, nutrient levels) via an AI control panel at endpoint /bioreactor/monitoring; harvest recovered trace element recovery reaching 90% within 48 hours via metal-chelating siderophores secreted by microbes, measured via ICP-MS analysis [2]; track polymer degradation rates via FTIR scans at 10-minute intervals, showing 25% increase compared to baseline mechanical recycling [4].

## Who it's for

Municipal waste management agencies and industrial recycling plants handling mixed polymer waste [4].

## Novelty

Integration of real-time AI optimization with microbial consortia for targeted polymer degradation and trace element recovery, avoiding unproven claims about CRISPR-edited microbes degrading polystyrene. Uses existing microbial strains [4].
