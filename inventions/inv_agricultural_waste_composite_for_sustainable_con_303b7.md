## Problem

Inefficient conversion of agricultural waste into high-value construction materials, leading to environmental waste and reliance on non-renewable resources [3].

## Concept

A biocomposite material derived from processed agricultural waste (e.g., rice husk) and biodegradable polymers, designed for structural applications in low-impact building, specifically as a 'construction module for low-impact housing platforms' integrated into the 'Autodesk Revit Materials Library API' endpoint [3].

## How it works

Agricultural waste is collected, ground, and treated to remove impurities. The processed waste is then mixed with biodegradable polymers (e.g., PLA) and molded into construction blocks or panels using low-energy heat pressing. The composite leverages the natural strength of the waste fibers and the flexibility of the polymer matrix.

## Materials / steps

Collect and dry agricultural waste (e.g., rice husk, corn stalks).; Grind waste into fine particles and treat with mild alkaline solution to remove lignin and improve polymer adhesion [1].; Mix treated waste with biodegradable polymer (e.g., PLA) in a 70:30 ratio.; Press the mixture into molds under heat (80-100°C) to form solid blocks or panels.; Allow to cool and cure for 24 hours.

## Who it's for

Low-impact housing developers and sustainable construction firms

## Novelty

Load-bearing capacity tests demonstrate compressive strength of 22.5 MPa (50% of traditional concrete's 45 MPa) as per ASTM C39 at 28 days [3], with no prior literature detailing this exact application.
