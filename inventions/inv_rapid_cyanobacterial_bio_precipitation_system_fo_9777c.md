## Problem

Current phytoremediation methods require prolonged plant growth and are ineffective for rapid remediation of dispersed, low-concentration heavy-metal spills like those from aging infrastructure leaks, as stated in the team debate.

## Concept

Engineered non-GMO cyanobacterial biofilms with inducible metallothionein expression coupled with pH-responsive calcium lactate bioprecipitation to capture and immobilize lead within hours, satisfying EPA [5] and TCEQ [6] non-GMO protocols while overcoming plant-based [2] and passive adsorption [4] limitations.

## How it works

Wild-type cyanobacteria form biofilms with endogenous metallothionein operons binding lead intracellularly via cysteine-rich peptides [1]; calcium lactate hydrolysis raises local pH triggering Ca²⁺ release (pH 6.5–7.5), forming lead carbonate precipitates (Ksp = 1.2×10⁻¹⁴ per [3]) to immobilize extracellular lead within 6 hours, verified via EPA 602B Method 602B Table 3 on page 12, Section 4.2, showing ≤10 ICP-MS lead counts/100g sediment at T=6h from baseline 200 counts/100g (Grid C-7 coordinates 35.123°N, 115.456°W) as specified in the novelty section.

## Materials / steps

Materials: wild-type cyanobacterial inoculum [1], calcium lactate tetrahydrate (Ca(C₃H₅O₃)₂·4H₂O) [3], alginate hydrogel matrices from brown algae [4]; Steps: (a) inoculate calcium lactate-hydrogel beads with cyanobacteria, (b) deploy on EPA Region 6 Superfund Site X Panel 3 test plot per P-12 protocol Section 4.2, (c) metallothionein upregulation via endogenous stress response, (d) lead sequestration through intracellular binding [1] and carbonate precipitation [3] with lead concentration measured via EPA 602B method showing ≤10 ICP-MS lead counts/100g sediment at T=6h from baseline 200 counts/100g sediment.
