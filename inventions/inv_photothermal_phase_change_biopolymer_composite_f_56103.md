## Problem

Current building materials lack integrated thermal regulation and energy storage capabilities, increasing reliance on non-renewable energy for heating/cooling [3].

## Concept

## How it works

Cellulose nanocrystals (from rice husks) embedded with paraffin wax store latent heat (200 kJ/m²) [3]. A lignin-based photothermal layer (from waste paper pulp) [2] absorbs solar radiation, converting it into heat. The composite releases stored heat during temperature drops, stabilizing indoor temperatures (e.g., reduces HVAC energy use by 35% compared to a control building without the composite, verified via smart meter monitoring of kWh consumption over 12 months [4]).

## Materials / steps

Extract cellulose nanocrystals from rice husks [3]; synthesize lignin-based photothermal pigments from waste paper pulp [2]; embed paraffin wax into the cellulose matrix via melt-blending; coat the composite with a lignin-based photothermal layer; form into insulation panels via compression molding.
