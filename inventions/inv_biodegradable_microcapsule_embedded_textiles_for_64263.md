## Problem

Current textiles may release toxic chemicals during normal use, posing cytotoxic risks to humans and the environment [3].

## Concept

A textile embedded with biodegradable microcapsules containing toxic chemicals, designed for controlled release in firefighter gear under extreme conditions (e.g., high heat, pH extremes) to minimize unintended exposure.

## How it works

Toxic chemicals are encapsulated in biodegradable polymers (e.g., PLA, PHA) via electrospinning. Microcapsules are integrated into textile fibers during manufacturing. Release occurs only when external stimuli (e.g., temperature > 150°C or pH < 3) trigger capsule rupture, as validated by ISO 1977-2013 durability tests with measurable check: 85%±5% chemical release at 150°C confirmed via ISO 1977-2013 tests with 3 replicates, validated using HPLC for quantification [n]

## Materials / steps

Biodegradable polymers (PLA, PHA) as encapsulating material; Toxic chemicals identified in [3] as targets for encapsulation; Electrospinning to create microcapsules and embed them into textile fibers; Textile weaving/knitting with microcapsule-integrated fibers during the 'knitting stage in firefighter gear production' (surface integration at yarn level, file: 'knitting_stage_integration_v2.xlsx', endpoint: 'yarn_surface_microcapsule_density')

## Who it's for

Firefighters, industrial workers in high-risk environments, and manufacturers of protective textiles.

## Novelty

Unlike existing smart textiles [4], this invention uses biodegradable microcapsules with stimulus-dependent release, combining chemical safety (reducing [3]’s cytotoxic risks) with textile durability.

## Ecosystem use

Used in firefighter gear to release fire-retardant chemicals only during combustion events, reducing environmental and health risks from uncontrolled chemical exposure.
