## Problem

Existing phytoremediation and bioprecipitation methods for heavy metal (e.g., Pb²⁺, Cd²⁺) remediation are limited by slow uptake rates and incomplete precipitation, leaving residual contamination in soil and water [2][3]. Current technologies, such as nanofibrous materials for photodegradation, are chemical-dependent and lack scalability for large-scale environmental restoration [4].

## Concept

A hybrid system using genetically modified plants (e.g., *Arabidopsis thaliana*) engineered to secrete metal-specific chelating peptides (e.g., phytochelatins) and symbiotic *Serratia marcescens* microbes, which bind and precipitate heavy metals more efficiently than standalone phytoremediation or bioprecipitation methods.

## How it works

1. Genetically modified plants secrete phytochelatins into soil, chelating heavy metals. 2. Engineered *Serratia marcescens* microbes absorb the chelated metals, precipitating them as insoluble metal sulfides or hydroxides. 3. Precipitated metals are trapped in microbial biofilms, reducing soil/water contamination.

## Materials / steps

Genetically modify *Arabidopsis thaliana* to overexpress phytochelatin synthase genes (using CRISPR/Cas9) [1]; engineer *Serratia marcescens* to express metal-binding proteins (e.g., metallothioneins) [3]; co-culture plants and microbes in controlled bioreactors to optimize chelation-precipitation synergy; deploy in contaminated industrial sites in Region X via soil inoculation or hydroponic systems, with measurable success defined as reducing Pb levels by 40% in soil samples via ICP-MS analysis within 6 months. Monitoring endpoint: Pb reduction to 40% in soil samples (ICP-MS) at 6 months [5].

## Who it's for

Environmental remediation agencies, industrial sites with heavy metal contamination, and agricultural regions affected by mining or manufacturing waste.

## Novelty

Combines synthetic biology-enhanced phytoremediation with microbial bioprecipitation, a synergy not explicitly described in prior art [2][3]. Unlike nanofibrous photodegradation systems [4], this method is self-sustaining and integrates plant-microbe interactions. Measurable endpoint: 40% Pb reduction via ICP-MS at 6 months [5].
