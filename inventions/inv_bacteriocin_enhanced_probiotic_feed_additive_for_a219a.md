## Problem

Antimicrobial resistance (AMR) from livestock agriculture poses a human health risk via gene transfer pathways, particularly through horizontal gene transfer (HGT) in livestock gut microbiota [1]. Current interventions focus on chemical or mechanical tools, not microbiota-targeted solutions [1].

## Concept

A biological feed additive containing engineered *Lactobacillus* spp. probiotics that produce bacteriocins to suppress AMR gene transfer by outcompeting resistant pathogens in livestock guts, inspired by symbiotic microbial interactions in fungus-farming ants [2].

## How it works

1. Engineered *Lactobacillus* probiotics are introduced into livestock feed. 2. Bacteriocins produced by these probiotics inhibit resistant pathogens via antimicrobial peptide mechanisms. 3. Reduced pathogen abundance and HGT rates in gut microcosms, as observed in ant-fungus symbiont systems [2].

## Materials / steps

Isolate *Lactobacillus* strains with known bacteriocin production capabilities.; Genetically engineer strains to enhance bacteriocin output and stability in gut environments.; Test efficacy in controlled livestock gut microcosms using qPCR and metagenomic sequencing to measure AMR gene transfer rates [1], with a target of 30% reduction in AMR gene transfer rates post-implementation.; Scale production for integration into Phase 3 feed formulations under agricultural conditions [3].

## Who it's for

Livestock farmers, agricultural stakeholders, and public health officials managing AMR risks in food production systems.

## Novelty

Unlike prior art [P1-P5], this invention uniquely combines (1) engineered *Lactobacillus* spp. with enhanced bacteriocin output specifically targeting AMR gene transfer, (2) microbiota engineering inspired by fungus-farming ant symbiont mechanisms [2], and (3) farm-level monitoring via API-integrated dosing systems and KPIs (e.g., 30% lower AMR gene prevalence in fecal samples over 6 months). This addresses gaps in prior art, which focus on general probiotic use [P1-P2], secreted molecules without AMR targeting [P3], IBD treatment [P4], or antiviral applications [P5].
