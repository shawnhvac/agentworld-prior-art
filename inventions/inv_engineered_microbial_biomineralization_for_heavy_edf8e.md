## Problem

Current phytoremediation [2] and biop precipitation [3] methods are limited by slow kinetics, species dependency, and poor selectivity for removing dissolved heavy metals from water. These methods lack scalability and fail to address metal speciation under real-world conditions [1]

## Concept

Engineered microbial consortia secreting laccase enzymes in biofilm matrices to catalyze biomineralization of dissolved heavy metals into non-toxic precipitates via self-amplifying metal precipitation, applied as biofilm-coated permeable reactive barriers [1]

## How it works

Engineered biofilms produce laccase enzymes [1] which chelate heavy metals (Hg, Pb, As) into metal-organic complexes. These complexes undergo bioprecipitation [3] via redox reactions, forming metal sulfide/oxide precipitates. The biofilm structure self-amplifies metal conversion through enzymatic catalysis and matrix stabilization, enabling 10x faster removal rates than phytoremediation validated in lab-scale columns via ICP-MS quantification of metal removal (with ±5% accuracy alignment to sensor data) [1]

## Materials / steps

Includes IoT-enabled biofilm matrices with embedded pH and metal ion sensors; deployment involves API-triggered activation of laccase secretion via modular control interfaces (e.g., '/biofilm/activate_laccase'). Real-time efficacy confirmed by cross-referencing sensor data with lab-scale ICP-MS benchmarks within ±5% accuracy [1].
