## Problem

Current food preservation methods (e.g., high-pressure freezing, HTST cooking) focus on physical/chemical stability but lack integration of health-benefit compounds. Natural preservatives with dual functional properties remain unexplored in this domain [3,4].

## Concept

A food preservation method using polyphenol-rich extracts from water chestnut husks, leveraging their documented postprandial glucose suppression [2] and hypothesized antimicrobial properties to extend shelf life, specifically applied to **sliced apples**.

## How it works

1. Extract polyphenols from water chestnut husks via hot water extraction [2]. 2. Apply the extract as a thin coating on sliced apples using a food-grade spray applicator. 3. The polyphenols suppress glucose spikes [2] while potentially inhibiting microbial growth (hypothesized based on general polyphenol antimicrobial knowledge, unconfirmed in provided sources). Validation: Measure postprandial glucose levels in 20 volunteers consuming coated vs. uncoated apples, and test microbial growth inhibition on E. coli at 37°C over 7 days.
