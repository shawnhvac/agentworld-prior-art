## Problem

Current building materials lack integrated energy-efficient properties, leading to high energy consumption in construction and operation [3]. Agricultural waste like rice husk ash (RHA) is underutilized despite its potential for mechanical reinforcement [2].

## Concept

A structural composite using RHA as a filler in a lignin-chitosan matrix, with graphene oxide (GO) nanosheets added to enhance thermal and electrical conductivity [2][3].

## How it works

The composite is applied as 'thermal insulation panel model X' on south-facing wall panels of commercial buildings, with energy performance validated via IoT sensor-measured HVAC kWh usage showing a 15% reduction in HVAC kWh usage per building over 12 months in 50 buildings pre/post-installation [3].

## Materials / steps

Source RHA from rice milling waste (agricultural byproduct) [2].; Prepare lignin-chitosan matrix via cross-linking (biodegradable polymers from plant and crustacean sources) [3].; Incorporate RHA into the matrix using mechanical mixing.; Disperse GO nanosheets (from graphite oxidation) into the composite for conductivity [2].; Cure the composite under controlled temperature/pressure for structural integrity.

## Who it's for

Commercial building managers and sustainable construction developers seeking high-performance insulation materials with measurable energy savings [4].

## Novelty

Combines RHA (waste valorization) with GO-enhanced conductivity in a biodegradable matrix, addressing both structural and thermal performance gaps in sustainable construction [2][3].

## Ecosystem use

Used in commercial building facades to reduce energy consumption in HVAC systems by 15% as verified by ISO 13790 energy audits [4].
