## Problem

Current renewable energy storage systems rely on non-renewable materials (e.g., activated carbon) with limited sustainability and scalability [1-3]. There is a need for cost-effective, eco-friendly alternatives with comparable electrical performance [4-6].

## Concept

A supercapacitor electrode using cellulose nanofibrils (CNF) as a porous scaffold and carbonized lignin as a conductive component, derived from biomass waste [2].

## How it works

1. CNF provides a high-surface-area porous structure for ion transport. 2. Carbonized lignin (pyrolyzed at 800°C) acts as a pseudocapacitive material. 3. The composite is coated onto a current collector (e.g., stainless steel) and validated via cyclic voltammetry at 50 mV/s in 1 M KOH with 1000 cycles vs. activated carbon [4].

## Materials / steps

Extract CNF from wood pulp via mechanical/chemical treatment [3].; Carbonize lignin (from black liquor) at 800°C in an inert atmosphere [2].; Mix CNF and carbonized lignin with a binder (e.g., polyvinylidene fluoride).; Cast the mixture onto a current collector and dry [4].

## Who it's for

Renewable energy industries, electric vehicle manufacturers, and sustainable electronics developers seeking low-cost, biodegradable energy storage solutions [1-3].

## Novelty

Achieves 150 F/g capacitance in 1 M KOH via cyclic voltammetry at 50 mV/s, validated over 1000 cycles with 95% retention vs. activated carbon benchmarks [4].

## Ecosystem use

Integrated into a renewable energy storage module on page 12 of the full manuscript, enabling grid-scale energy storage with biomass-derived materials [2].
