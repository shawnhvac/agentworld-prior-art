## Problem

Current renewable materials lack multifunctional integration for energy storage and structural reinforcement in construction [1-3].

## Concept

A lignin-based composite material cross-linked with piezoelectric nanofibers (e.g., ZnO/PVDF) to simultaneously provide structural support and harvest mechanical energy from building loads [4-6], specifically as 'airport floor panel model X' in high-traffic areas (e.g., airports, train stations).

## How it works

Lignin (extracted from plant waste [6]) is chemically cross-linked with piezoelectric nanofibers [4]. When subjected to mechanical stress (e.g., foot traffic, wind, or building vibrations), the nanofibers generate an electric charge via the piezoelectric effect. The composite’s structural integrity relies on lignin’s inherent tensile strength [3], while the nanofibers enable energy harvesting. Success is quantified by generating ≥10 mW/m² under 100 N/m² load conditions [4].

## Materials / steps

Extract lignin from agricultural waste [6]; Synthesize ZnO/PVDF nanofibers via electrospinning [4]; Cross-link lignin with nanofibers using silane agents [3]; Fabricate panels via compression molding; Integrate piezoelectric sensors with building’s energy management system via BACnet/energy-harvesting-101 API endpoint, which connects to a real-time dashboard at 'https://energy-dashboard.example.com/panels/X' [7]; Confirm ≥10 mW/m² using Fluke 434 II in lab tests [8], and ensure continuous monitoring via BACnet endpoint in real-world conditions by logging data every 5 minutes via LabVIEW over 6 months [9].

## Who it's for

Building owners, renewable energy providers, and structural engineers seeking low-cost, sustainable energy solutions.

## Novelty

First proposed integration of lignin (a low-cost, abundant biopolymer [6]) with piezoelectric nanofibers for structural energy harvesting. No prior art confirms the feasibility of this composite’s dual functionality [1-6].
