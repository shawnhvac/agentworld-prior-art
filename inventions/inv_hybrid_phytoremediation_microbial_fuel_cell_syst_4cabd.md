## Problem

Contamination from heavy metals (e.g., lead, arsenic) and toxic organic compounds in soil and water remains a global challenge, with existing bioremediation methods limited by slow pollutant uptake rates and lack of energy recovery [3][4].

## Concept

A self-sustaining system combining engineered phytoremediation (using hyperaccumulating plants) with microbial fuel cells (MFCs) to simultaneously remove pollutants and generate electricity from organic contaminants.

## How it works

1) Hyperaccumulating plants (e.g., *Thlaspi caerulescens*) absorb heavy metals from soil/water via roots. 2) Concurrently, MFCs use electrogenic bacteria to oxidize organic pollutants (e.g., petroleum hydrocarbons), producing electricity. 3) Plant roots and MFC anodes are spatially integrated to share rhizosphere microbial communities, enhancing pollutant degradation through synergistic interactions [2][4].
